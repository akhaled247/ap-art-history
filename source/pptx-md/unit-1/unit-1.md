<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Running Horned Woman is an example of parietal art\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">This means any prehistoric artwork fixed to a natural cave wall\, floor\, or ceiling\. Derived from the Latin word paries \(wall\)\, it includes cave paintings\, engravings\, petroglyphs\, and relief sculptures\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Contrast this with portable "mobiliary art" like small figurines or the Apollo 11 Stones\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Apparent hierarchy through contrasting scales of figures</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Possibly showing a goddess\, or a ritual or ceremonial dance</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Twisted perspective as in Hall of Bulls at Lascaux\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Extensive use of diagonals\, dots and lines create a sense of rhythm and movement to the work\. </span>

![](img%5C__Unit%201%20Global%20Prehistory_29.png)

# What is grog?

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Grog is a granular material that's added to clay to improve its properties and structure\. It's also known as chamotte or firesand\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Grog is typically made by grinding fired clay at high temperatures\, but it can also be made from sand\, crushed brick\, natural minerals\, or calcined minerals\.</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Grog helps clay dry and fire more easily\, reducing shrinkage and cracking\. </span>
