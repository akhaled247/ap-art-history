# Key Perspectives on the Birth of Judaism:

# Abraham (The Patriarchal Age, c. 2100–1800 BCE): Judaism is often traced to Abraham, who recognized one God, initiating the covenantal relationship.
Moses (The Exodus, c. 1313 BCE): Many Jewish traditions identify the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai as the official birth of the nation and its religious laws.
The Babylonian Exile (6th Century BCE): Historically, many scholars believe the, codified religion (Judaism) emerged after the destruction of the First Temple in 586 BCE, when Judeans in exile defined their identity through text and law.
Second Temple/Hellenistic Period: The formalization of "Judaism" as a distinct religion, with the Torah as a central authority for the public, took place during the Persian or Hellenistic periods.

# In 632, a group of prominent early followers of Islam elected Abu Bakr, father in law and a companion of Mohammed, to be the first caliph, or leader of the Islamic community. This was over the objections of those who favored Ali ibn Abi Talib, Mohammed’s cousin and son-in-law (named the 4th caliph in 656 CE).

Caliphate (“Khilafat” in Arabic) was a semi-religious political system of governance in Islam, in which the territories of the Islamic empire in the Middle East and North Africa and the people within were ruled by a supreme leader called Caliph.


---


# Caliphate: A political and religious system of government where the leader, or caliph, is the successor to the Prophet Muhammad. 

Dynasty: A line of leaders who ruled the Muslim lands that made up the caliphate. 

Partial List of Caliphates:
Rashidun Caliphate (632–661)
Umayyad Caliphate (661–750)
Abbasid Caliphate (750–1517)
Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171)
Ottoman Caliphate (1517–1924)


---


# The Abbasids were a Muslim dynasty that rose to power by overthrowing the Umayyad Caliphate in the 8th century, primarily due to grievances like the Umayyads' perceived corruption, heavy taxation, and discrimination against non-Arab Muslims (mawali), while the Abbasids claimed a closer lineage to Prophet Muhammad through his uncle Abbas, which gave them a strong political and religious claim to rule; this conflict resulted in the Abbasid Revolution and the establishment of the Abbasid Caliphate.

Despite civil wars, assassinations and infighting between the groups over the legitimate leaders, Islam spreads across Northern Africa. After the assassination of nearly all the Umayyad princes in 749, Abd al-Rahman I flees to the Iberian Peninsula, where he establishes the Umayyad dynasty.

# Books during this time period were hand-made, luxury items. 

The Vienna Genesis is one of the oldest surviving illustrated biblical books. 

It is named for its current location in Vienna, Austria and the book of the bible that it represents, Genesis.

The term Genesis means generation or creation.


---


# It is believed that the book was created in Syria or Constantinople during the early 6th century. 

 In the 5th century, while the western provinces were enduring massive German invasions which eventually led to the end of Roman rule there, the inhabitants

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Books from this period became increasingly decorative as a result of the new design \(bound pages\) and durable materials such as vellum and parchment\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Books produced prior to the invention of the printing press are usually referred to as illuminated manuscripts\. The term </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> _illumination _ </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">simply means to brighten or adorn\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Medieval Illuminated manuscripts were written by hand and “illuminated” with gold and silver\.</span>

# 51. San Vitale

![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_88.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Ravenna\, Italy\. Early Byzantine Europe\. c\. 526–547 ce\. Brick\, marble\, and stone veneer; mosaic\. 5 images \(this and the next 2 slides\)</span>

![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_89.png)


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">San Vitale is located in Ravenna\, in northern Italy\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Ravenna was the seat of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century and then of Byzantine Italy until the 8th century\. It has a unique collection of early Christian mosaics and monuments\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">All eight Unesco\* buildings – the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia\, the Neonian Baptistery\, the Basilica of Sant'Apollinare Nuovo\, the Arian Baptistery\, the Archiepiscopal Chapel\, the Mausoleum of Theodoric\, the Church of San Vitale and the Basilica of Sant'Apollinare in Classe – were constructed in the 5th and 6th centuries\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">\*United Nations Educational\, Scientific and Cultural Organization</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">While it was built during the reign of Justinian \(527\-565\)\, there are many aspects about San Vitale that remain uncertain\, including the precise date of its construction and its connection to Constantinople\. However\, it is worth noting that it was begun when Ravenna was under </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Ostrogoth</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> rule\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Work on the church began under Bishop Ecclesius \(521\-532\) after returning from Constantinople and was financed by a Ravenna banker named Julianus Argentarius\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The church\, though\, was consecrated much later by Bishop Maximian in 547\. Bishop Victor \(538\-545\) probably played a substantial role in the construction of San Vitale\, as his monograms can be found on the impost blocks of its capitals\.</span>


---


![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_90.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The building is designed based on a central plan\. That is\, a building in which the sides are of equal length and in which the main space is symmetrical when bisected laterally and longitudinally\. A centrally\-planned building may be square\, circular\, or polygonal\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The location of Hagia Sophia has been the site of several structures\. <span style="color:#fce5cd">The revolt was suppressed by Justinian\, with the encouragement of Theodora\, and some 30\,000 members of the rioting political factions were killed\. </span>

# Much of what is known about the creation of the Lindisfarne Gospels comes from a 10th-century inscription (a colophon) added roughly 250 years after the book was made. This note credits four specific individuals with its production:
• Eadfrith (The Scribe and Artist): A monk who served as the Bishop of Lindisfarne from 698 to 721. Scholars widely agree that the manuscript's 250 intricate pages are the work of this single hand. He spent years engineering the geometric layouts using lead-point compasses, ruler grids, and handmade pigments.
• Ethelwald (The Binder): Eadfrith’s successor as bishop, who oversaw the original heavy leather binding of the book.
• Billfrith the Anchorite (The Metalworker): A hermit craftsman who adorned Ethelwald's casing with an outer layer of gold, silver, and precious gems.
• Aldred (The Translator): A 10th-century priest who later added an interlinear translation. He wrote a word-for-word Old English translation between the original Latin text, creating what is recognized as the oldest surviving copy of the Gospels in the English language.

# On June 8, 793, a fleet of Viking longships landed on Holy Island. Because monasteries were pacifist centers of wealth and entirely undefended, the raiders systematically plundered the priory. This brutal attack sent shockwaves through Christian Europe and is widely cited by historians as the official beginning of the Viking Age.

Remarkably, the manuscript itself survived the initial 793 chaos, and a small monastic community continued to live on the island for several decades. However, as broader Viking invasions intensified across northern England, the island became permanently unsafe.

# Gothic Style

# The Goths were a supposed “barbaric” people who held power in various regions of Europe during the Middle Ages. c. 5th-8th centuries.

Having been a nomadic people, the Goths were not known for architecture.

The name Gothic was applied to a style of architecture by individuals and historians after the fact.

The Gothic style was a grand departure from previous architectural styles that had preceded it.

The Gothic style came after the Romanesque style.


---


# Gothic Style

# Relative peace, prosperity and stability allowed for new, grand and experimental building programs between roughly 1000 - 1400 CE. 

The Gothic period is particularly notable as essentially a French architectural innovation.

It moved from France throughout Europe with each country adding its own particular Gothic aesthetic vision.


---


# Gothic Style

# In France the pursuit was for a vertical cathedral interior which would mirror the “heavenly city of God”, this led to innovations in structural elements.

The ribbed vault allowed for the interior space to be lightened both architecturally and aesthetically.

These vaults opened interior space and created a visual presentation that was light, airy and filled with stained glass windows.

These windows created an interior program that reflected the tone of the period.

# Gothic Style

# One of the most notable characteristics of Gothic are the pointed arches. 

Late Romanesque architecture began to experiment with pointed arches and ribbed vaulting. 

Possibly influenced by Islamic architecture from the Iberian Peninsula.

Pointed arches reduced outward thrust, and allowed for slimmer columns/piers.


---


# Gothic Style

![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_264.png)

# Slimmer columns gave a lighter feel than those of Romanesque churches. 

The same feel was repeated in the nave gallery and clerestory, and helped to convey a heavenly sense of space that extended from the floor to the vault.


---


# Gothic Style

![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_265.png)

# Reduced thrust and slimmer columns allowed for more and larger windows, bringing in more light than ever before. 

As the Gothic style grew, so did the ornamentation of the buildings.

Complex designs adorn the windows, tracery, and carvings of these buildings.

Even the ribbed vaulting grew increasingly complex in their designs.

# A term coined by Giorgio Vasari (1511–74) for 13th-century Italian painting that was influenced by Byzantine styles. The maniera greca is characterized by gilded backgrounds, flattened space, and striations to suggest folds of fabric.

'Maniera greca'' is Italian for '' in the Greek style.''

The Italo-Byzantine icon style is usually said to have become common after the Sack of Constantinople in 1204 by the Fourth Crusade. The booty brought back to Europe included many icons, which probably stimulated demand for more.

![](img%5C1of2%20__Unit%203%20Early%20Europe%20%26%20Colonial%20Americas_330.png)

# The Italo Byzantine style was the dominant style in Italian painting until the end of the 13th century, when Cimabue and Giotto began to take Italian, or at least Florentine, painting into new territory. But the style continued until the 15th century and beyond in some areas and contexts.

Cimabue is today credited for instigating a move towards naturalism in art, shifting away from the rigid and stylized forms that defined much of medieval art.

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Alhambra was built by the last Islamic dynasty to rule Spain\, the Nasrid Dynasty \(1232\-1492\)\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The name Alhambra means the red fort or castle\. This is from the color of materials used that give a reddish appearance\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The location is high upon a hill which makes it good for defensive purposes\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">1 mile of walls combined with thirty towers of varying size enclose this city within a city\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The palatine city housed six palaces and two palace towers\, of which only those called Comares and Leones remain\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">In addition to a residence for the ruler and close family; there was the citadel; barracks for the elite guard who were responsible for the safety of the complex\, and an area called medina \(or city\)\, near the Puerta del Vino \(Wine Gate\)\, where court officials lived and worked\.</span>

# The Renaissance

# The Renaissance arrived at different countries at varying times- Italy was the first to experience this movement beginning in the 14th century while the it did not reach England until the sixteenth century. A general consensus among historians is that by the early 17th century, the Renaissance had come to an end.

There is a shift in belief systems/worldviews in Europe that will have a dramatic impact on future generations. 

This impact could be seen in architecture, art, literature, mathematics, music, philosophy, politics, religion and science.

Increased trade and the labor shortage caused by the Black Death gave rise to something of a middle class.

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Humanism</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> in the Renaissance</span>

# Renaissance Humanism was an intellectual movement typified by a revived interest in the classical world and studies which focussed not on religion but on what it is to be human.

People began questioning the systems they were living in. Humanists believed people should be educated in classical art, literature, and science.

They also believed that God gave humanity great potential and that humans should make the most of it rather than blindly following a religious plan.

Interest in humanism, a philosophy that emphasized the individual and the human capacity for fulfillment through reason, transformed the Renaissance artist from an anonymous craftsman to an individual practicing an intellectual pursuit.

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Spread of the Renaissance</span>

# The movement first expanded to other Italian city-states, such as Venice, Milan, Bologna, Ferrara and Rome. Then, during the 15th century, Renaissance ideas spread from Italy to France and then throughout western and northern Europe.

The Northern Renaissance differed from the Italian Renaissance in its focus: the Italian Renaissance emphasized classical humanism, while the Northern Renaissance was more focused on Christian Humanism, social reform, and the religious implications of the printing press.


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Spread of the Renaissance</span>

# Italian Renaissance: Drew heavily from classical antiquity, with an emphasis on classical themes, proportion, and realism. Fresco and tempera were common mediums. 

Northern Renaissance: Known for its detailed realism and naturalism, often depicting domestic interiors with a focus on material objects. Artists pioneered the use of oil paints to achieve luminous colors and intricate textures. The North also saw a greater proliferation of printmaking (woodcuts and engravings) compared to the South.


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Spread of the Renaissance</span>

# Italian Renaissance: Contributed to political centralization and the development of diplomacy within Italian city-states. 

Northern Renaissance: Played a key role in the Protestant Reformation, partly due to the printing press allowing for the mass distribution of critiques of the church and the translation of the Bible into vernacular languages. The rise of a merchant class and international trade also influenced its economic and social landscape.


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Spread of the Renaissance</span>

# Oil paint (developed in the 7th century CE) traveled from Afghanistan to Northern Europe through the Silk Road, with artists on the ancient trade route spreading the technique westward. This knowledge was then rediscovered and developed in Europe, where artists like those in the Early Netherlandish painting movement, such as Jan van Eyck, significantly advanced oil painting methods by the 15th century, leading to its widespread adoption.

While oil paint originated and proliferated in Northern Europe due to its unique properties and compatibility with local artistic traditions, it did not take hold in Italy until much later. Italy's established traditions in fresco and tempera painting, alongside distinct artistic goals, initially delayed its adoption.
