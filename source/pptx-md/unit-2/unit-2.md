---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Uruk was an early and important Sumerian city state\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The main temple to the city’s god Anu\, the god of the sky\,  became the center of the city\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">There was a complex with administrative offices\, staff of priests/scribes\. It was a place of commerce and religious functions\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The White temple sat upon a ziggurat\. Ziggurats are similar to a truncated pyramid\. The temple’s height would have been roughly 40’ above street level and its corners are roughly aligned to the cardinal directions\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Mudbrick was a common building item due to the lack of stone\. As a result\, erosion was a common problem\. </span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The ziggurat’s interior core was made from sun baked bricks\, while the exterior was made up of fired brick\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Bitumen is a natural\, sticky\, tar\-like petroleum substance used as mortar or a waterproof coating between bricks\. In 3500 BC\, bitumen was primarily obtained from natural surface seepages where it bubbled up directly from the ground\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Plaster and lime was often used to coat and decorate the outer walls of buildings and ziggurats\. </span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Individuals would </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">climb the staircase to the ramp\, proceed around the terrace and the temple\. Entrance would be gained through the side of the temple with a bent axis approach to the altar\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The temple’s size was roughly 57’ x 73’ and not made for corporate worship\. </span>

# Egyptian Dynastic and Kingdom Periods

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> __To simplify things\, you basically have the early periods\, the Kingdom periods and the intermediate periods\.__ </span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> _Predynastic Period_ </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc"> \(c\. 5000\-3100 B\.C\.\) \- Unification of Egypt and the </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc">development of hieroglyphics occurred\. </span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> _Archaic \(Early Dynastic\) Period_ </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc"> \(c\. 3100\-2686 B\.C\.\) \- Mastabas predate pyramids\, and an Egyptian “God King” ruled a unified Egypt\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The distinctive hallmarks of ancient Egyptian civilization\, such as art\, architecture and many aspects of religion\, took shape during the Early Dynastic period\.</span>


---


# Egyptian Dynastic and Kingdom Periods (cont.)

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> _Old Kingdom: Age of the Pyramid Builders \(c\. 2686\-2181 BCE\) _ </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc">\- This is the first peak of Egyptian civilization in achievement and complexity\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The distinction between ED/OK is the revolutionary change in architecture and the effects that large\-scale building projects had on Egyptian society and economy\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The period is known for the large number of pyramids constructed\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The oldest is the Step Pyramid of Djoser in Saqqara</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The Seated Scribe\, Giza Pyramids and King Menkaura and Queen are all from the Old Kingdom period\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">This time of peace/prosperity/trade allowed for the pursuit of artistic endeavors\.</span>


---


# Egyptian Dynastic and Kingdom Periods (cont.)

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> _Middle Kingdom: 12th Dynasty \(c\. 2055\-1786 B\.C\.\)_ </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc"> \- Renewed consolidation of power to central government occurred during the 11th \- 12th Dynasties\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">Reliefs \- sculpture\, carvings meant to be seen primarily from the front\, were often used on buildings\. This included both high reliefs and bas or low reliefs\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">Many Middle Kingdom monuments are poorly preserved\, which contributes to the era’s relative lack of modern prominence\. Mudbrick construction of many temples has resulted in their loss\. </span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The canon remains\, but with some interest in increased naturalism\. </span>


---


# Egyptian Dynastic and Kingdom Periods (cont.)

<span style="color:#fff2cc"> _New Kingdom \(c\. 1567\-1085 B\.C\.\)_ </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc"> \- The was Egypt’s most prosperous time\, when it was at the height of its power\. It was also the time of some of Egypt’s most famous Pharaohs\.</span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The Amarna period was also during the New Kingdom\. This was a very innovative\, albeit short time period in which the arts deviated from strict </span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc">adherence</span>  <span style="color:#fff2cc"> to standard Egyptian artistic canons\. This was a time when there was a distinct change in artistic style\. </span>

<span style="color:#fff2cc">The Temple of Amun Re and Hypostyle Hall\, Akhenaten\, Nefertiti\, and three daughters\, Tutankhamun’s tomb\, Last judgment of Hunefer\, page from the Book of the Dead\, are all from this period\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The pottery of the ancient Greeks is important both for the beauty of its forms and decoration and for the light it sheds on the development of Greek pictorial art\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Few or no Greek works in wood\, textile\, or wall painting have survived and because fired clay pottery is highly durable\, it has become the main source of information about how the Greeks gradually solved the issues of representing three\-dimensional objects and figures on a flat or curved s</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">urface\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The large number of surviving works would indicate a heavy reliance on pottery for daily life\. </span>


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_243.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Polygnotus was an ancient Greek painter from the middle of the 5th century BCE\. Unfortunately\, none of his works are still in existence today\. We know about him through the accounts and writings of others\. </span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The influence of Polygnotus seems to be evident in the Niobides </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Krater</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Greek paintings traditionally featured figures next to each other on the same level\. Polygnotus changed that by introducing multiple seating levels for his forms \(complex composition\)\. This new method led to new conventions for describing spatial relations within a painting\. According to these\, the lower rows of a picture were nearer and the upper rows were farther from the viewer\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Krater moves away from Isocephalism: A peculiarity in the design of bas relief by which the heads of human figures are kept at the same height from the ground\, whether the characters are seated\, standing\, or mounted\. </span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_244.png)


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_245.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Greek pottery developed from a Mycenaean tradition\, borrowing both pot forms and decoration\. The earliest stylistic period is the Geometric\, lasting from about 1000 to 700 BCE\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Proto\-Geometric: In this period the surface of the pot was completely covered with a network of fine patterns in which circles and arcs predominate\. This abstract decorative vocabulary was later enriched by such devices as the meander \(key pattern\)\, checker\, triangle\, herringbone\, and swastika\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The succeeding true Geometric style is characterized by these forms and by the gradual appearance of animal and finally human figures\. These too were geometrized\, being given angular silhouettes and arranged symmetrically\, usually in strips around the pot\. </span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_246.png)

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_247.png)


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_248.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Greatly expanded Greek trading activities during the late 8th and early 7th centuries BCE led to a growing Eastern influence on Greek pottery painters\. This phase is first apparent in works made in Corinth in about 700 BCE\. At this time Asian motifs found their way onto all makes of Greek pots\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">New subjects appear \- monsters as the sphinx\, siren\, griffin and animals as the lion\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Corinthian painters created a silhouette technique in which figures painted in the characteristic black slip were incised with thin lines to show detail\.</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Athenian painters adopted the black\-figure pottery style around 630 BCE but emphasized human figures rather than animal motifs as pictorial themes\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">From 600 BCE on\, Athens increasingly became the dominant centre for Greek pottery\, eventually exporting its ware throughout the Mediterranean world\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">It was during this period that the practice of signing of pots by potters and painters first became common\.</span>

_[Making Greek Vases](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WhPW50r07L8)_

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_249.png)


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The ancient Greeks used a three\-stage firing process that consisted of a cycle of oxidizing\, reducing\, and re\-oxidizing the atmosphere inside the pottery kiln\. This three\-stage process was necessary to achieve the lustrous black gloss of the slip against the natural or augmented color of the clay\.</span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_250.png)


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Here's a more detailed explanation of the three stages: </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd"> __Oxidizing Stage:__ </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">The kiln is heated with oxygen present\, causing the clay to turn red\-orange\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd"> __Reducing Stage:__ </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Oxygen is removed from the kiln\, and the temperature is raised\, causing the slip \(a liquid clay\) to turn black\, creating the background\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd"> __Re\-oxidizing Stage:__ </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Oxygen is reintroduced into the kiln as it cools\, allowing the areas without slip to return to their original red\-orange color\, creating the figures\.</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Red\-figure pottery\, invented at Athens about 530 BCE\, is just the reverse of the black\-figure style in that the reddish figures appear light against the black background of the pot surface\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Around 500 BCE Greek artists abandoned the convention of using only profile views and began to use three\-quarter frontal poses as well as foreshortening and the carefully depicted overlapping of one figure on another\.</span>

# Alexander the Great’s Empire

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Alexander the Great reigned as King of Macedon from 336 BCE to 323 BCE\, a period of 13 years during which he conquered a vast empire and spread Hellenistic culture across parts of Europe and Asia\. # Alexander the Great’s Empire (cont.)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">After Alexander the Great's death\, his vast empire fragmented into several smaller\, independent kingdoms\, known as the Hellenistic kingdoms\, as his generals fought over control of its territories\. <span style="color:#fce5cd">What is the significance of the House of the Vettii?</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The construction of the house and its decorations belong to the final period of Pompeii's occupation and therefore provides important evidence of the aesthetics of the city on the eve of its destruction\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">It was not only a place to live\, but a place to conduct business and entertain guests\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The house played a key part in the reinforcement of social hierarchy\, as the patron received clients in the atrium\.</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The patron/client system can be defined as a mutual arrangement between a person that has authority\, social status\, wealth\, or some other personal resource \(patron\) and another who benefits from their support or influence \(client\)\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The patron might receive political support\, military service and more from the client\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Clients were free men who were under legal obligation to a patron who had bestowed some benefit upon them\. While not all clients were former slaves who had been freed by the patron or his father\, when a slave was freed\, he automatically became his former master's client\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The house of the Vettii  was owned by two brothers\, who were freedmen\. They  were former slaves who became wealthy wine merchants and rose to prominence in society\. </span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The house is considerable in size and covers an area of approximately 11\,840 square feet\.</span>

---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_343.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The atrium was the center of the owner's social\, political\, and business activities\. Since most Roman Atrium houses did not have windows facing outside the atrium was also an important light source\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">A peristyle surrounded the courtyard\. The columns were of the Doric order\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The center was open to the sky to allow for light and smoke to escape as well as rain to pass through\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">A small pool or an artificial reservoir used for swimming or as a fish pond or other water collection\.</span>

# First or Incrustation or Structural Style

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_346.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Dates: ca\. 200 BCE \- 60 BCE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Colorful blocks that resemble marble slabs</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Molded into raised backs to aid in the marbling effect</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Frequently used the Doric order</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">These faux finishes are still influential today</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Colorful\, but could have the effect of making small rooms even more claustrophobic</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Late Republican period drawing on Hellenistic painting and architecture</span>


---


# Second or Architectural Style

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Dates: ca\. 60 BCE \- 20 BCE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Attempts at a realistic feel and tends to open up spaces</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Uses everyday objects and scenery in a fairly realistic way</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Renderings of windows looking onto an outdoor scene</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Incorporated columns painted on wall to frame those views</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Metal and glass objects were used in the paintings</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Attempts were made at linear perspective\, but still show a lack of total understanding</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">There is a movement away from the public scenes to private decor as the end of the architectural style approaches</span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_347.png)


---


# Third or Ornamental Style

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_348.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Dates: ca\. 20 BCE \- 20 CE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">There is a rejection of opening up the space \(illusion\) and a movement toward ornamentation</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Large areas of monochromatic color with small renderings of framed architecture</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Division of wall space with painted slender columns or candelabras</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">This allowed for repainting of small areas instead of repainting entire walls</span>


---


# Fourth or Intricate Style

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Dates: ca\. 20 CE \- 79 CE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Architectural elements in unrealistic settings with flattened space and skewed perspectives</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Contrasts the simplicity of the ornamental style with chaotic rooms filled to excess</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">It revives large\-scale narrative painting and panoramic vistas\, while retaining the architectural details of the Third Style</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">A combination of the three styles that came before\. Faux marble blocks along the base of the walls\, as in the First Style\, frame the naturalistic architectural scenes from the Second Style\, which in turn combine with the large flat planes of color and slender architectural details from the Third Style\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Pantheon’s dome is the largest in the ancient world\, 142 feet across\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Built with concrete using various size and weights of rock to control strength and weight of the dome</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The oculus \(27’ in diameter\) helps with weight concerns but also provides  light for the interior\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Walls are some 20’ thick to help bear the weight and outward thrust of the dome\.</span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_427.png)


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_428.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The frieze above the columns has a series of false windows with pediments alternating with rectangular shapes\. These do not align with the coffered ceiling\, which creates a dynamic feel\, almost as if the dome is rotating\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Rectangles\, squares and geometric shapes are used throughout the interior</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">Originally built by Marcus Agrippa in 27 BC\, son\-in\-law of Emperor Augustus\) in 27 BCE\.; burning in 80 CE\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Rebuilt or restored in the same location by Emperor Domitian \(emperor from 81\-96 CE\) ; but struck by lightning and burned again in 110 CE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">It was thought that Emperor Hadrian was ruler when construction began on the final version\, but new research has shown that it might have been his predecessor\, Trajan\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Pantheon has been in near continuous use since its final construction in c AD 126\-128</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The Pantheon fell into disrepair\, as did many Roman buildings\, after Constantine moved the center of the empire from Rome to Constantinople\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">With the rise of </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Christianity</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">\, the building was abandoned and pillaged by the Goths</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">In 609 CE\, it was dedicated as a Christian church\, possibly saving it from ruin as the church spent a great deal repairing and maintaining the structure\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">It was the first Roman temple to be consecrated as a church\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Pantheon continues to function as a church\, as well as a major tourist destination\. </span>

# 47. Ludovisi Battle Sarcophagus
Late Imperial Roman. c. 250 ce. Marble.

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_431.png)


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_432.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">In 192 CE\, the reigning </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Caesar</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> Didius Julianus was was assassinated\, and Rome was once again plunged into </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">civil</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> war\. After a succession of 4 generals\, the 5th\, Septimius Severus\, finally musters control and rules until his death in 211 CE\. </span>


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_433.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Chariot Procession of Septimius Severus from the The Arch of Septimius Severus\, 203 CE</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Later Imperial art moved away from earlier Classical influences\, and Severan art signals the shift to art of Late Antiquity\. The characteristics of Late Antique art include frontality\, stiffness of pose and drapery\, deeply drilled lines\, less naturalism\, squat proportions\, and lack of individualism\. This style foreshadowed late antique and Byzantine art\.</span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">By the 3rd century\, burial of the dead in Rome was becoming more popular than cremation\. As a result\, Sarcophagi were becoming more </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">prevalent\. </span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_434.png)


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">One such sarcophagus was discovered in 1621 and purchased by Cardinal Ludovisi\. </span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">Ludovico Ludovisi \(22 or 27 October 1595 – 18 November 1632\) was an Italian cardinal and statesman of the Roman Catholic Church\. He was an art connoisseur who formed a famous collection of antiquities\, housed at the Villa Ludovisi in Rome\.</span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_435.png)


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">The front of the sarcophagus is a chaotic battle scene depicting the Romans fighting with the barbarians\, most likely the Goths\.</span>

<span style="color:#fce5cd">This illustrates rejection of the naturalism of the Classical period\. The scene is packed with figures with no clear horizon line\. </span>

![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_436.png)


---


![](img%5C__UNIT%202%20Ancient%20Mediterranean_437.png)

<span style="color:#fce5cd">The central figure is presumed to be the son of Decius\, Roman emperor from 249 to 251\. He is the only Roman without a </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">helmet</span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd"> and he has a small “x” on his forehead\. This is thought to be a </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">Mithras </span>  <span style="color:#fce5cd">from a Persian religion\, showing an emblem of truth and victory over death\. \(Ironically\, Decius and his son both died in a battle with the Goths\, but that’s another story\.\) </span>


---


<span style="color:#fce5cd">This work was created after the glory days of Rome had passed and the Roman army was far from invincible\. So\, perhaps this piece was meant to hearken back to better times\. </span>
